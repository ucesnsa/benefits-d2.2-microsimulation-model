Is my service worth what it costs, and could we cope if unemployment rises? A plain-language edition of the D2.2 Microsimulation Valuation Tool.

For social-service providers in

Two questions about your service, answered in plain numbers

You tell the tool what your service does, how many people it helps and what it costs to run. It answers two questions that funders, boards and ministries ask.

1. Is it worth what it costs?How much good your service does in a year, turned into money, compared with what it costs to run. For example: “about 1.15 back for every 1 spent”.
2. Could you cope if unemployment rises?If more people lose their jobs, more of them need services like yours. The tool estimates how many more would come to you, and whether your team has room.

How to use it

  1. Open Your service, choose your type of service and type in your own numbers. The answers update as you type.
  2. Read the first answer, Is it worth what it costs? It gives a best estimate and a range from low to high.
  3. Move the unemployment slider in the second answer to see how many more people would need you, and whether your team would have room.
  4. At the bottom, copy the summary into a report or funding application. It carries the range, not just the best estimate.

The first answer: is it worth what it costs?

The tool adds up the good your service does for the people it helps in a year, and puts a money value on it. It then divides that by your yearly running cost. The result reads as “X back for every 1 spent”. Above 1, the benefit is bigger than the cost. Below 1, it is smaller.

Where the benefit comes from

Extra incomeMoney that ends up in the pockets of the people you help. It is worked out with , a model of the tax and benefit system run on . A gain to a poorer household counts for more than the same gain to a richer one (see the settings below).
Costs avoidedProblems your service prevents that would otherwise cost money, such as an eviction or a homeless placement. Each is valued at its published cost.
Feeling betterHow much better people’s lives are, measured by how satisfied they say they are with life on a scale from 0 to 10. One point higher, for one person, for one year, is valued at . That value comes from the UK Treasury’s official guidance on valuing wellbeing.

Why there is a range

For most services, how much they help comes from published research, often from another country. So each figure has a low, a best estimate and a high. When the research is thin the range is wide. When you report the result, give the range, not just the best estimate. The copyable summary does this for you.

When the tool can count only part of the value

Some things a service achieves have no reliable published figure yet, such as the relief a food bank brings beyond the food itself. The tool does not guess these. It counts them as zero and tells you so. For those services the answer is a floor: the true value is at least this much, not exactly this much. It is not a verdict that the service is not worth it.

The second answer: could you cope if unemployment rises?

You choose how much unemployment rises, in percentage points. A rise from 10% to 14% is 4 points. The tool estimates how many more people would lose their main job across , and scales that down to the size of your service to estimate how many more would come to you.

Your team’s capacity is your staff multiplied by the clients each can take a year. For example, 10 staff taking 50 clients each can help 500 people a year. The tool compares that with the people you help now plus the extra people, and shows how full your team would be:

Room to spareunder 100% full
Full, no room to spareexactly 100% full
Over capacitybetween 100% and 120% full
Well over capacity120% full or more

If you would be over capacity, the tool tells you how many people you could not take, and what it would cost to help them at your cost per person.

The model works out rises of 3 and 5 points directly. Smaller rises are scaled down from 3 points, and rises above 5 points are a straight-line extension, so treat those as a rough guide.

The settings you can leave alone

Under More settings there are four things, already filled in. Change them only if you have a reason to.

Who you mainly helpThe poorest 30% of people, the next 30% on low-to-middle incomes, or a mix of everyone. It changes the extra-income and costs-avoided parts of the value, because the same service moves incomes differently for different groups, and gains to poorer people count for more.
Cost per person helpedUsed to price any extra people you could not take. We fill in an estimate for each service, most of them borrowed from another country. If you know your own cost per person, type it in: your figure is better than ours. It applies to that service only, and “Use our estimate” puts ours back. For services counted per household, such as food aid or housing, divide your household cost by your average household size first.
Extra weight for poorer householdsHow much more a 1 gain counts when it goes to someone poorer. “None” counts every 1 the same. “Standard” is the tool’s usual setting, and the stronger settings count gains to poorer people for more. It affects the extra-income and costs-avoided parts of the value. It does not affect the feeling-better part.
Income pattern borrowed fromTo value the extra income your service brings, the tool borrows the way a comparable national policy changes incomes for the same kind of people. By default this is . Change it only if another policy on the list is a closer match for what your service does to people’s incomes. The list holds only policies that raise incomes; the full tool also lists shocks, downturns and cuts, which would make a service’s income effect negative.

How sure are we?

  • These are estimates, built from published research and national survey data, not measurements of your own service.
  • The second answer is a national rise in unemployment scaled to the size of your service. It is not a forecast for your area.
  • Everything is per person helped. Costs and outcome values are at 2024 prices; extra income is at prices, the year of the model’s income data, exactly as in the full tool.
  • On the Your service page, How sure are we? tells you where each number for your chosen service comes from, and See the full working shows every figure behind the answers.

About this tool

BENEFITS (Building Economic, Needs-Based and Environmental evaluation Frameworks for Inclusive Transformation of Social services in Europe) is a Horizon Europe project coordinated by KMOP, with partners across Europe. Deliverable D2.2 is its Microsimulation Valuation Tool, built for the United Kingdom, Spain, Italy and Greece.

This is the @@COUNTRY_NAME@@ edition of its provider part, rewritten in plain language. It uses the same data and the same calculation as the full tool, so for the same inputs every figure is the same. It is a single file that runs offline in any web browser, with nothing to install and no login. It holds figures only, no personal data, and what you type stays on your computer.
